# PLE-SBS print-only scale model

A working tabletop model of the slidewire and of my add-on, the switch yard: one wire tied to a window, three pods, and on the floor a receiving end where self-indexing points send each pod into its own braked bay. Every part except the wire is 3D-printed on a Bambu Lab P1S.

- Parts: `stl/` (22 files), generated by `make_parts.py`; preview in `parts_preview.png`
- Bill of materials and print settings: [BOM.md](BOM.md), `parts.csv`
- Sketches: `sketch_1_room_layout.png` to `sketch_6_terminations.png`, generated by `make_drawings.py`
- Print pack for the Bambu Lab P1S: `print_pack/phase1/` and `print_pack/phase2/`, each with `stl/`, `sheets/` (a dimensioned PNG per part) and `sliced_p1s/` (a sliced `.gcode.3mf` per part); `print_pack/README.md` has the slicer's exact grams and times; `phase1.zip` and `phase2.zip` are the same folders for download. Built by `make_print_pack.py`.
- Phase 2, the pad and rocket: `phase2_pad.md`, `BOM_phase2.md`, `stl_phase2/`, `render_pad*.png`

## How it works

![Room layout](sketch_1_room_layout.png)

The wire runs from a printed hook on a window grill or handle down to the handover module on the floor, 4 to 6 m away with about 1 m of drop. Pods wait in three start gates clamped near the top. Flick a gate lever and the pod rolls down on two V-groove wheels, over the handover ramp, onto a printed monorail blade.

![Yard plan](sketch_2_yard_plan.png)

On the turnout plate the blade splits. A pivoting tongue sends the pod down one of three lanes that fan out at 6°. Each lane is a bay: a lead rail, a brake comb (twelve flexible teeth that the pannier floor presses flat), a chain bed (the pod's hook picks up a print-in-place chain and drags it), and a zig-zag bumper as the backup stop.

![Mechanism](sketch_4_mechanism.png)

The self-indexing points: after the pod has fully left the tongue, its top strikes the cross-bar of a four-arm star. The star turns a quarter turn. On the same shaft, under the plate's parts, a four-dwell cam sets the tongue for the next pod: bay 1, then 2, then 3, then 2, then 1 again. A leaf spring on the plate holds the tongue's follower against the cam; a detent finger on a post holds the star at each quarter turn. No power, no sensors, exactly as in the paper, except that the full-size design indexes with a ratchet at the points and the model indexes with a star 70 mm past them.

![Pod](sketch_3_pod.png)

A pod is two identical plates, two wheels on printed snap pins, and two pannier halves that slide onto dovetails on the outside of the plates. The pod straddles the rail blade, so the panniers hang either side of it. Hook lips under the wire keep the trolley on; the rail blade passes through the 3.2 mm slot between them.

## Sizing to the room (tie point 2 m high)

`layout.py` sizes the wire to the room: `.venv/bin/python scale_down/layout.py --tie 2.0 --floor 8.0`. The parts do not change; only where the wire is tied and how far the yard sits from it.

With a 2 m tie point the height is not the limit, the floor is. The worked example's slope (300 m span over a 45 m drop, about 1 in 6.7) at a 2 m drop would need a 13 m span and a 2.5 m yard, so the true-to-scale options are:

| Scale | Span | Drop | Floor needed | Ride | Arrival |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1:50 | 6.0 m | 0.90 m | 7.7 m | 3.3 s | 3.5 m/s |
| 1:35 | 8.6 m | 1.29 m | 10.7 m | 4.0 s | 4.1 m/s |
| 1:25 | 12.0 m | 1.80 m | 14.8 m | 4.8 s | 4.6 m/s |

Recommendation: stay at 1:50 (the printed parts as they are), tie the wire 0.96 m above the floor for the to-scale run, and use the rest of the 2 m for an experiment the paper could not do on a real pad: keep the span at 6 m and raise the tie point in steps (0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 1.2, 1.5, 1.9 m). Each step is a different crew access height at the same span, which is one row of Figure 4's sizing map (25 to 97 m full size). Measure the arrival speed and whether the comb stops the pod inside its bay; the slope where it no longer does is the model's "stop too hard" edge, and the shallowest slope where a light pod still reaches the yard is its "stall" edge.

If the floor is shorter than 7.7 m, shorten the span and accept a steeper wire; a 4 m span with a 0.9 m drop is about 1 in 4.4, the left side of the band in Figure 4, and the pods will arrive faster than scale.

## Build order

1. **Print and test one pod and one lead rail.** Put a wheel on a pin and spin it: it should turn freely with a little side play. Push the dovetail of a pannier onto a plate: firm, no wobble. If the fit is off, change `CLR` in `make_parts.py` (0.2 by default) and reprint the small parts.
2. **Assemble the pods.** Wheel between the two plates, pin through (head outside, snap end through the far plate), the locating pin on each plate into the other's hole. Slide the panniers on. Put the same coins in both halves.
3. **Print the yard.** Handover, turnout plate, three leads, three combs, three chain beds, three bumpers, three chains, tongue, indexer, four arms. Join everything with the dovetails in this order along each lane: turnout exit, lead, comb, chain bed, bumper. The turnout's entry slot takes the handover's tab.
4. **Fit the mechanism.** Tongue's root boss over the pivot post. Indexer over the cam post; the cam sits on the 4 mm boss. Push the four arms into the hub sockets so each cross-bar hangs toward the lanes (the arm leg is along the plate axis; one bar must wait at x = 240 across the three lanes). Turn the star by hand: the tongue should click through bay 1, 2, 3, 2. Check the tongue's tip lines up with each exit rail; if it overshoots, shave the follower boss, if it undershoots, add a layer of tape to it.
5. **Lay the chains.** Each chain in its trough, the first link hung in the stirrup ring.
6. **Rig the wire.** Thread the three trolleys onto the wire from its free end before terminating it. At the window: three turns round the capstan post, tail into the wedge pocket, push the wedge in, then tie a plain knot in the tail as well. Hang the hook. At the floor: lead the wire over the nose block, along the groove in the ramp head, down the dive hole, into the wedge cleat, and on to a heavy furniture leg where it ties off. Weight the turnout plate with books.
7. **Set the gates.** Clamp the three gates 0, 150 and 300 mm from the hook, keys pushed in, levers up. Load the pods behind them.
8. **Run.** Set the star so the tongue points to bay 1. Flick gate 1; when the pod has stopped, flick gate 2; then gate 3. Pod 1 to bay 1, pod 2 to bay 2, pod 3 to bay 3, each stopped by its comb with the chain dragged behind it. Reset: lift the pods out, lay the chains back, turn the star to bay 1.

## Tuning

- **Too fast into the bumper:** add ballast (friction in the comb grows with weight), or print the comb's teeth at 1.5 mm (`tooth` in `make_parts.py`).
- **Stops in the lead:** too much ballast or teeth too stiff; snip every second tooth.
- **Pod stalls on the wire:** more drop, or less wire tension sag; the real system has the same problem, that is the point of Figure 4 in the paper.
- **Tongue does not switch:** the leaf spring is not pressing, or the detent finger is holding too hard. Bend the spring in a little; thin the finger.
- **Star does not turn:** the pod arrives too slowly for the detent; move the gates higher or lighten the detent.
- **Derails at the handover:** the wire must lie flush in the head groove and the ramp head must be level with the wire top; add a layer of tape under the nose block.

## Build log and test results

- `results.csv` is the run log: one row per release with pod mass, span, drop, time to the handover, time to a stop, the bay reached, whether the points indexed correctly, and the stop distance in the bay. The simulator's Scale model tab fits μ and the drag area from the `release_to_handover_s` column.
- The switch-yard test is pass/fail per run: pod 1 to bay 1, pod 2 to bay 2, pod 3 to bay 3, points indexed by the pod itself, every pod stopped in its comb before the bumper.
- Section 4 of the paper carries the summary; the bracketed sentence at its end is where the fitted μ and the measured-versus-predicted plot go once `results.csv` has runs in it.

## What to measure

The protocol in `docs/scale-model-protocol.md` applies: time each run on slow-motion video, three repeats per setting, and fit the rolling resistance with the simulator's Scale model tab. New here: the time from release to a stopped pod in each bay, and the headway you can run between pods on the one wire with the yard against a single dock (hold pods 2 and 3 in bay 2 to imitate a dock).

## Honesty

This is a demonstration of the mechanism, not a calibrated brake. The brake comb is friction, the chain is too light to matter at this scale, and the bay lengths are compressed. The wire itself, the pods and the sag are to scale (1:50, Froude), and those are what the protocol measures. The star-and-cam indexer is my model-scale substitute for the ratchet at the points in the paper.
